Inside IS House: designing for shade

The brief began with a refusal — no corridors, and no curtains. In Bandung that is less a style preference than a climate problem.

In Bandung the low afternoon sun reaches deep into any room facing west. Most houses answer this with fabric. We prefer to answer it with geometry, because a curtain is a decision you have to make every day and a roof plane is a decision you make once.

One volume, cut in the middle

A single 32-metre volume runs east to west, cut through the middle by a courtyard. That cut does three things at once: it removes the need for corridors, it gives every room two aspects, and it lets hot air leave the plan at its centre rather than travelling the length of the house.

At noon the interior sits entirely in shadow. At six the light arrives sideways, low and warm, and the travertine does the rest.

“A curtain is a decision you make every day. A roof plane is a decision you make once.”

Drawn here, built by people we know

Everything fitted was drawn in the studio and built by one joinery workshop in Yogyakarta — the seven-metre kitchen bench, the library wall, the beds. Full-size prototypes were made before anything reached site, which is why the house has no site-decided details.

  • Every junction drawn at 1:5 before tender
  • One workshop, one site foreman, weekly studio presence
  • Twenty-six months from brief to handover

Delivered at the budget band agreed in the first month — which, for a house of this size, is the part the client remembers.
